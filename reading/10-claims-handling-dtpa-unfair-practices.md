## Key facts to watch

Discrepancies and unsupported cards are quoted from the research notes and the sourced answer. The note says what the key got wrong. The sourced answer is the version to learn.

### CARD-0311 — Key discrepancy

*Deceptive Trade Practices Act, what is it unlawful for a consumer to do?*

Key ('drop charges filed against agent unless they have $5,000,000 in assets') garbles the DTPA; Bus. & Com. Code §17.45(4) excludes business consumers with assets of $25 million or more from 'consumer' status.

Sourced answer: Under the Texas DTPA, a 'consumer' who may sue does not include a business consumer with assets of $25 million or more (or owned/controlled by an entity with $25 million or more). The deck's '$5,000,000 assets' wording is a garbled version of this exception.

### Other sourced Texas figures on cards graded correct

These matched the key and a citation. They are here because they are easy to mis-memorize. The full wording is in `data/final_answers.csv`.

- `CARD-0541` Which Texas statute lets an insurer submit a reservation of rights instead of immediately  — Texas Insurance Code §541.060(a)(4) (failing within a reasonable time to affirm or deny coverage OR submit a reservation of rights); also 28 TAC §21.203(10).
- `CARD-0562` An insurer receives all requested items on a claim but, without explanation, pays 75 days  — Delay beyond 60 days (when no other statute sets a period) makes the insurer liable for damages under §542.060 — interest/penalty on the claim plus reasonable attorney's fees.
- `CARD-0563` Which rule says an acknowledgment of a claim within 15 business days is presumed reasonabl — 28 TAC §21.203(2) (unfair claim settlement practices rule).
