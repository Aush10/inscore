# 01. Insurance basics and contract law

**179 cards.** Two sittings if you need them: vocabulary and valuation first, then the contract and the kinds of insurers. One sitting is enough if the self-check is clean.

Source decks: 108 from card sharp qa.txt, 28 from ch6-10.txt, 27 from review.txt, 16 from 100 questions.txt.

This section is the card-sharp glossary plus the contract and market cards that the later decks repeat. It covers risk, perils, hazards, loss, indemnity, valuation, the parts of a policy, what makes a contract, and how insurers are classified in Texas. Texas pages lead. Mike Russ chapter 1 and the IRMI glossary are general-concept reading only: they explain the vocabulary, and they are a California course and an industry glossary, not Texas law.

Sourced answers are in [`data/final_answers.csv`](../data/final_answers.csv). The original key is [`data/answer_key.txt`](../data/answer_key.txt). Its header says the original answers are unverified. Where this file marks a key discrepancy, study the sourced answer.

## Key facts to watch

Discrepancies and unsupported cards are quoted from the research notes and the sourced answer. The note says what the key got wrong. The sourced answer is the version to learn.

- CARD-0133 is the market card to slow down on. County mutuals, Lloyd's plans, and reciprocals are licensed Texas insurers. Since 2003, Insurance Code §2251.003 applies rate regulation to Lloyd's plans, reciprocal exchanges, and county mutuals for most property and casualty lines. The key's 'non-admitted and no rate regulation' list does not match that.

### CARD-0110 — Unsupported

*Binder*

The deck's '30 days, rarely more than 60' binder duration: no Texas source found (Ins. Code §549.055 only requires a lender-accepted binder to be replaced within 30 days).

Sourced answer: A binder is temporary (oral or written) evidence of coverage until the policy is issued. It is short-term; the deck says written binders usually run 30 days and rarely more than 60.

### CARD-0133 — Key discrepancy

*What types of insurances companies are non-admitted and have no rate regulations?*

The key calls county mutuals, farm mutuals, reciprocals, Lloyd's plans, RRGs and purchasing groups 'non-admitted/no rate regulation.' County mutuals, Lloyd's and reciprocals are licensed Texas insurers, and since 2003 Ins. Code §2251.003 applies rate regulation to Lloyd's plans, reciprocal exchanges and county mutuals for most P&C lines.

Sourced answer: Per the deck: county mutuals, farm mutuals, reciprocal exchanges, Lloyd's plans, risk retention groups and purchasing groups (historically exempt from Texas rate regulation). Current Texas law: county mutuals are exempt from most insurance laws (Ins. Code §912.002), but since 2003, Lloyd's plans, reciprocal exchanges and county mutuals are subject to rate regulation for most P&C lines (§2251.003). These are licensed Texas insurers, not 'non-admitted'; RRGs are regulated mainly by their domicile.
